# The Constitution of Collective-State Inference (CSI)

**Version:** 0.1 (Foundational Draft)

## Preamble

Collective-State Inference (CSI) is a scientific framework for studying how latent conditions of bounded collectives may be inferred from observable evidence generated through collective dynamics.

The purpose of this Constitution is not to define computational methods or prescribe implementation details. Those evolve over time.

Instead, this Constitution establishes the enduring principles that define the identity, scope, governance, and scientific commitments of the CSI research program.

Every future handbook revision, research paper, dissertation chapter, software implementation, and empirical investigation should remain consistent with these principles unless formally amended through the theory governance process.

## Article I — Purpose

CSI exists to provide a scientifically grounded framework for inferring latent collective conditions while preserving theoretical, epistemological, and methodological rigor.

The framework seeks to advance scientific understanding of collectives rather than promote any particular computational technology.

## Article II — Scope

CSI addresses the problem of estimating latent states whose referent is a bounded collective.

CSI does not define individual psychological measurement, general organizational analytics, predictive modeling alone, social network analysis alone, large language models, multi-agent simulation, or any single machine-learning architecture. These may contribute to CSI implementations but do not define the framework itself.

## Article III — Foundational Commitments

### Section 1 — Collective Primacy

The primary inferential object of CSI is the latent state of a bounded collective. Participant-level observations may contribute evidence. They are not themselves the inferential target.

### Section 2 — Construct Primacy

Every CSI investigation begins with a theoretically specified collective construct. Constructs are defined independently of the computational models used to estimate them.

### Section 3 — Level Integrity

Scientific claims must remain aligned with the level to which they refer. Evidence gathered at one level requires explicit theoretical justification before supporting inference at another.

### Section 4 — Evidence Accountability

Observable information becomes evidence only when theory or accumulated empirical knowledge justifies its relationship to the target construct. Prediction alone does not establish evidentiary relevance.

### Section 5 — Representation Neutrality

No representation—including aggregation, relational modeling, temporal modeling, embeddings, or multimodal fusion—shall be presumed either sufficient or insufficient before empirical evaluation. Representation adequacy is a scientific question rather than a theoretical assumption.

### Section 6 — Algorithmic Neutrality

CSI defines an inferential problem rather than a computational architecture. No statistical model, machine-learning algorithm, neural architecture, or symbolic reasoning system is inherently CSI. Algorithms implement CSI. They do not constitute CSI.

### Section 7 — Scientific Falsifiability

CSI shall make claims that permit empirical evaluation and potential refutation. The framework is strengthened through rigorous testing rather than protected from criticism.

### Section 8 — Theoretical Transparency

Definitions, assumptions, propositions, and conceptual changes shall remain explicit and traceable. The evolution of the framework should be documented rather than implied.

## Article IV — Canonical Theory

The CSI Theory Handbook serves as the canonical conceptual specification of the framework. The handbook defines ontology, epistemology, inferential framework, measurement, validation, canonical terminology, and theoretical propositions.

Individual papers should derive from the handbook rather than redefine its foundations independently.

## Article V — Theory Governance

The CSI framework evolves through documented scholarly review.

Substantive conceptual changes shall be accompanied by a Theory Decision Record (TDR) documenting the proposed change, scientific rationale, alternative formulations considered, expected consequences, compatibility with existing theory, and adoption status.

This process preserves conceptual continuity while allowing scientific progress.

## Article VI — Canonical Terminology

Each foundational CSI concept shall possess one canonical definition maintained within the Theory Handbook. Alternative terminology may be discussed historically or comparatively but shall not replace canonical definitions without formal review.

This article exists to prevent conceptual drift across publications.

## Article VII — Relationship Between Theory and Evidence

The Constitution recognizes that theory and empirical evidence inform one another.

Empirical findings may support existing propositions, constrain their scope, motivate refinement, or motivate new propositions. No single empirical study automatically redefines the framework. Likewise, theoretical propositions require empirical evaluation before they become established scientific knowledge.

## Article VIII — Research Program

The CSI research program distinguishes among four classes of scholarly artifacts:

1. **Constitution** — defines enduring foundational commitments.
2. **Theory Handbook** — defines canonical conceptual specifications.
3. **Research Papers** — develop, operationalize, evaluate, or extend portions of the framework.
4. **Applications** — implement the framework within particular domains or computational systems.

Each artifact serves a distinct role.

## Article IX — Stewardship

CSI is intended as a cumulative scientific framework.

Future researchers should be able to understand why theoretical decisions were made, reproduce conceptual definitions, compare competing formulations, and extend the framework without unnecessary conceptual divergence.

Accordingly, stewardship of the framework includes preserving clarity, documenting evolution, and encouraging constructive scholarly criticism.

## Article X — Foundational Proposition

> **Collective-State Inference is a scientific framework for estimating theoretically defined latent conditions whose referent is a bounded collective, using observable evidence interpreted through explicit theoretical, representational, inferential, and validation principles.**

This proposition expresses the enduring identity of the framework independently of any specific computational implementation.

## Closing Statement

The purpose of this Constitution is not to conclude the development of Collective-State Inference.

Its purpose is to ensure that future development proceeds coherently.

Scientific frameworks become durable not because they cease to evolve, but because they evolve transparently, cumulatively, and with conceptual discipline.

This Constitution establishes those commitments for the CSI research program.
