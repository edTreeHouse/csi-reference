# TDR-001 — Collective State as the Primary Inferential Target

**Status:** Accepted

## Decision

The primary inferential target of Collective-State Inference is a latent state whose referent is the bounded collective rather than the attributes or states of individual participants.

## Scientific Rationale

Participant-level observations may provide evidence about a collective, but CSI is specifically concerned with claims about the condition of the collective itself. Maintaining this distinction protects level integrity and prevents individual-level states from being treated as collective-level states without a defensible composition argument.

## Alternatives Considered

### Treat individual and collective states as interchangeable targets

Rejected because it collapses levels of analysis and obscures the theoretical referent of the inference.

### Infer only participant states and aggregate afterward

Retained as a legitimate baseline or composition strategy when theoretically justified, but rejected as the defining formulation of CSI.

## Consequences

CSI studies must explicitly specify the collective boundary and target collective construct. Participant-level evidence remains permissible but must be theoretically linked to the collective-level inferential claim.

## Backward Compatibility

Compatible with the existing CSI framework and canonical definition.
