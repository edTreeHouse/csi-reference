Scholarly quality assurance

Citation Verification Audit

A public record of how bibliographic details, stable identifiers, and source-to-claim relationships are checked across the CSI Reference.

Audit 1.123 registry records reviewedUpdated August 7, 2026Living quality-control record
The audit strengthens traceability; it does not convert the CSI framework into peer-reviewed or empirically validated research. Every CSI-specific definition and proposition retains its stated research status.

Scope

The initial audit reviewed 17 works in the Scholarly Sources registry. An August 7, 2026 supplemental novelty review added six contemporary computational neighbors, bringing the registry to 23 records. Across both review rounds, author names, publication year, title, source title, volume, issue, pages or article number, editors and publisher where applicable, and DOI or other stable identifier were checked against authoritative records.

DOI-linked records were checked against DOI registration or publisher records; books and chapters were checked against publisher catalog or table-of-contents records. The six contemporary additions were checked against OpenReview, Springer Nature, arXiv, Elsevier/Crossref-linked records, ACM proceedings records, and the King's College London research repository as appropriate to publication status.

Source-to-claim review

The audit also reviewed the principal places where each work is used in the CSI Reference. The test was whether the cited source directly supports the attached scholarly principle—for example, composition models, collective constructs, construct validity, dynamic emergence, situation awareness, human–AI interaction, probabilistic calibration, group-state modeling, tempo-relational team modeling, collective dynamics, Group Awareness Tools, or Group Conversational Agents—or whether the text clearly identifies the statement as a CSI synthesis or original proposition.

This is a targeted evidentiary audit rather than a new systematic literature review. It does not imply that every source endorses CSI as a framework, nor that a source supports claims beyond the scope described in the article text.

Audit outcomes

  • No DOI mismatch was identified among the DOI-linked registry records reviewed.
  • The Kozlowski and Klein chapter record was expanded to include the book's full subtitle.
  • The Sawyer (2000) chapter record was expanded to include its verified page range.
  • The August 2026 supplement added six 2025–2026 records directly relevant to CSI novelty boundaries.
  • The new sources were used to narrow, rather than inflate, CSI priority claims: contemporary systems already model group states, team constructs, collective dynamics, and group-oriented AI interaction.
  • The registry displays its current verification date and links to this audit record.
  • Automated validation checks that DOI links use canonical https://doi.org/ form and that article citation anchors resolve to registry records.

How to interpret verification

Bibliographically verified means that the public record matches the available authoritative metadata or, for preprints and accepted/in-press work, the authoritative repository record available at the time of review. Claim-use reviewed means that the source is used for a scholarly principle within its apparent scope. Neither label means that the source validates CSI's original definitions, inferential chain, governance requirements, or proposed system architecture.

Ongoing maintenance

Future additions to the registry will undergo the same checks before being described as verified. Publication status for preprints and accepted/in-press work will be updated when a version of record becomes available. Corrections will be recorded in the Version History. Readers may report a bibliographic or interpretive concern through ed@collectivestateinference.org.

Audit method and correction handling are governed by the Editorial and Citation Policy.
