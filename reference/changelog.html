Versioning policy

How changes are recorded

Major editions identify meaningful changes to the CSI construct system, qualification requirements, methods, or research status. Minor article versions identify substantive clarifications within an edition. Typographical and accessibility corrections may be deployed without changing an article version when they do not alter meaning.

The Editorial and Citation Policy governs source selection, claim classification, citation verification, corrections, and disclosure of generative-AI assistance.

Canonical alignment and journal submission

Canonical definition revised and foundational manuscript submitted

Date: August 11, 2026

  • Advanced RA-001 to Version 0.4 and aligned the living CSI Reference with the foundational manuscript submitted to Computational and Mathematical Organization Theory.
  • Revised the canonical definition so collective states are defined at the level of the collective rather than definitionally assumed to be irreducible to aggregation.
  • Preserved composition-model fit as the governing rule: additive or consensus composition may be appropriate for some constructs, while relational, dispersion, temporal, or process structure may be required for others.
  • Recorded non-reducibility as an empirical question rather than a definitional assertion.
  • Updated public manuscript status to Submitted — Technical Check. This status does not imply editorial acceptance or peer review.
  • Distinguished unpublished contemporary neighbors (Reading the Room and BEHAVE) from published literature while retaining them for novelty-boundary context.

Archival continuity: Edition 0.1 remains unchanged. These updates apply to the living CSI Reference.

Contemporary literature review

CSI positioning refined against contemporary literature

Date: August 10, 2026

The CSI Reference was updated following a review of recent computational research on group-level and collective-state modeling.

  • Clarified that aggregation can support a collective construct when justified by an appropriate composition model.
  • Expanded the contemporary literature base to include earlier computational work modeling group-level states.
  • Refined CSI's novelty position: CSI does not claim to be the first computational approach to modeling group states. Its proposed contribution is specifying the conditions under which computational evidence warrants interpretation as a latent collective state.
  • Updated supporting references and citation-verification records accordingly.

Archival continuity: These updates apply to the living CSI Reference. Previously archived releases remain unchanged.

Contemporary literature alignment

Novelty claims narrowed and supporting literature expanded

Date: August 7, 2026

The CSI Reference was strengthened through comparison with recent computational research on group states, team states, relational-temporal modeling, collective dynamics, group-awareness tools, and group conversational agents.

  • Preserved the canonical CSI definitions while strengthening their relationship to adjacent computational research.
  • Narrowed the novelty claim from priority over computational group-state targets to a general, level-aware theory specifying when computational representations warrant interpretation as latent conditions of bounded collectives.
  • Added contemporary evidence for both additive and relational-temporal composition strategies, preserving the position that non-reducibility is construct-dependent and empirically testable.
  • Expanded the supporting scholarly-source registry with close contemporary computational neighbors.

Archival continuity: These updates apply to the living CSI Reference. Previously archived releases remain unchanged.
